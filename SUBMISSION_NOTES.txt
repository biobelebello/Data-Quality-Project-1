Approach: I used the UCI Bank Marketing dataset (41,188 call records) from Week 3 and built a separate data-quality notebook. It profiles the raw data, logs every issue with a decision and rationale, cleans the data in one reusable function, validates the result with 12 automated checks, and exports a cleaned CSV, a data dictionary and a one-page decision brief.

Key decisions: I removed 12 exact duplicates. I found 12,716 hidden missing values coded "unknown" across six columns (the Week 3 quality check reported zero because its dtype test skipped pandas "str" columns, which I fixed). I kept "unknown" as its own category rather than dropping about 26% of rows or guessing values. I converted the pdays = 999 placeholder (96% of rows) to nulls. I resolved 4,110 rows where pdays contradicted previous by trusting previous. I excluded call duration from the decision analysis because it is only known after the call (target leakage). I kept high call counts but flagged them, and renamed columns so they are SQL-safe.

Results: The baseline subscription rate is 11.3%. Customers with a previous successful outcome convert at 65%, customers aged 60+ at 40%, students at 31% and retirees at 25%. Mobile converts at 15% against 5% for landline. Calls after the 3rd attempt take 26.5% of call volume but bring in only 12% of subscriptions. A priority segment covering 7.7% of customers delivers 29% of subscriptions. The brief recommends calling this segment first, capping attempts at 3, preferring mobile numbers, and testing the approach in a randomised pilot.

Assumptions and limitations: The data covers one bank in 2008-2010 and has no year column. The findings are associations, not causes. There is no cost or deposit-value data, so the analysis optimises conversion rather than profit. Duplicates are assumed to be data-entry repeats because there is no customer ID.

Help needed: I would welcome feedback on whether keeping "unknown" as a category is preferred over imputation for this module, and on how to bring cost data into the next iteration.
